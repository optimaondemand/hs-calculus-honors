Antiderivatives and Differential Equations
How do you recover a function from information about how it changes?
📖 WHAT THIS MODULE IS ABOUT
A derivative is a clue. A differential equation is a mystery about a function, told only through its rates. Lesson 15.01 runs differentiation backward: antiderivatives, the constant \(C\) and why it cannot be dropped, and the initial conditions that pick out one particular function, including the position of a moving particle recovered from its acceleration. Lesson 15.02 meets the differential equation itself, an equation that mentions a function and its derivative, and learns to read it as a slope field, a picture of the direction every solution must take. Lesson 15.03 solves the first great family, separable equations, by sorting the variables to opposite sides and integrating, and learns to say honestly which solutions division can lose. Lesson 15.04 closes the module with \(\dfrac{dy}{dt}=ky\), the equation behind growth, decay, half-life, and doubling time, and asks how far a model like that can be trusted. By the end of the module the derivative and the integral stand together as inverse operations that you can set to work on the real world.

💡 HOW TO SUCCEED THIS MODULE
Every answer in this module can be checked, and the checking is part of the work. After you find an antiderivative, differentiate it. After you solve a differential equation, substitute your function back into the equation and test the initial condition. When you separate variables, ask whether you divided by something that could be zero, because that is where a solution can quietly disappear. When you use a growth or decay model, write one sentence about what it assumes and where it would stop describing the world. Real understanding here looks like an answer you have tested and a limit you have named, not a formula you have only followed.

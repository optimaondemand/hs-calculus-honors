📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
When a function behaves nicely, the limit is just the value.
Lesson 1.01 asked you to feel your way toward a limit, one decimal place at a time. That patience is not always necessary. Most of the functions you will meet in this course, including every polynomial and most rational functions, are built from pieces that behave predictably near any point. Mathematicians have proved a small set of laws that let you skip the table entirely and simply substitute. Today you learn those laws, and exactly when substitution is allowed.

1
I will be able to state the basic limit laws: sum, difference, constant multiple, product, quotient, and power or root.
2
I will be able to evaluate a limit by direct substitution and explain why substitution is valid.
3
I will be able to identify which law justifies a given step in a limit computation.

📚 PART 1 · THE LAWS THAT LET US SKIP THE TABLE

A limit law is a proven guarantee, not a shortcut you are merely allowed to try. Each law below says that if the pieces of a function have limits, the combination has the limit you would expect. Let \( \lim_{x\to a} f(x) = F \) and \( \lim_{x\to a} g(x) = G \), both real numbers. Then:

▶
Sum and difference: \( \lim_{x\to a}[f(x)\pm g(x)] = F \pm G \)
▶
Constant multiple: \( \lim_{x\to a}[c\,f(x)] = c\,F \), for any constant \(c\)
▶
Product: \( \lim_{x\to a}[f(x)\,g(x)] = F\,G \)
▶
Quotient: \( \lim_{x\to a}\dfrac{f(x)}{g(x)} = \dfrac{F}{G}\), provided \(G \ne 0\)
▶
Power and root: \( \lim_{x\to a}[f(x)]^n = F^n \) and \( \lim_{x\to a}\sqrt[n]{f(x)} = \sqrt[n]{F}\), when the root is defined
▶
Composition: if \(g\) is continuous at \(F\), then \( \lim_{x\to a} g(f(x)) = g(F) \)

Two consequences follow at once. Because a polynomial is nothing but sums, differences, and constant multiples of powers of \(x\), the sum and product laws guarantee that for a polynomial, the limit as \(x\to a\) always equals the polynomial evaluated at \(a\). Because a rational function is one polynomial divided by another, the quotient law guarantees the same shortcut, with one condition attached: the denominator's limit must not be zero. That single condition is the seam this lesson is built around.

💡 DID YOU KNOW?
For over a century after Newton and Leibniz, mathematicians combined limits informally, trusting intuition about what "should" happen when quantities are added or multiplied. It was the French mathematician Augustin-Louis Cauchy who, in his 1821 lecture notes Cours d'Analyse, gave the limit a precise definition and proved that these combination rules actually follow from it. What had been a habit became a theorem.
📚 PART 2 · THE LAWS AT WORK

Worked example: a polynomial by direct substitution

Evaluate \( \displaystyle\lim_{x\to 2}(3x^2 - 5x + 1) \). Since this is a polynomial, the sum, difference, constant multiple, and power laws guarantee that substitution alone finds the limit.

\[ \begin{aligned} \lim_{x\to 2}(3x^2 - 5x + 1) &= 3(2)^2 - 5(2) + 1 \quad \textit{substitute } x=2 \\ &= 12 - 10 + 1 \\ &= 3 \end{aligned} \]

Worked example: a quotient built from given limits

Suppose \( \lim_{x\to a} f(x) = 6 \) and \( \lim_{x\to a} g(x) = -2 \). Find \( \displaystyle\lim_{x\to a}\frac{f(x)g(x) + 1}{g(x)} \). Here the values of \(f\) and \(g\) are never given, only their limits, so every step must cite a law.

\[ \begin{aligned} \lim_{x\to a}\frac{f(x)g(x)+1}{g(x)} &= \frac{\lim_{x\to a}[f(x)g(x)+1]}{\lim_{x\to a} g(x)} \quad \textit{quotient law, since } \lim g(x) = -2 \ne 0\\ &= \frac{(6)(-2)+1}{-2} \quad \textit{product law, then sum law}\\ &= \frac{-11}{-2}\\ &= 5.5 \end{aligned} \]

Worked example: a composition

Evaluate \( \displaystyle\lim_{x\to 4}\sqrt{2x+1} \). The inner function \(2x+1\) is a polynomial, so its limit at \(4\) is found by substitution; the square root is continuous wherever it is defined, so the composition law applies.

\[ \begin{aligned} \lim_{x\to 4}\sqrt{2x+1} &= \sqrt{\lim_{x\to 4}(2x+1)} \quad \textit{composition law}\\ &= \sqrt{9}\\ &= 3 \end{aligned} \]

Explain to a classmate: why is "substituting the number" not simply a shortcut you are permitted to try, but a conclusion guaranteed by a law every single time you use it? Point to a specific law in your answer.

⚠️ WATCH OUT
The quotient law has a guardrail: it requires the denominator's limit to be nonzero. What happens when both the numerator and the denominator approach zero at the same time? Substitution goes silent rather than giving a wrong answer, and a new technique is needed. That guardrail is exactly where Lesson 1.03 begins.
📚 PART 3 · NAME THE LAW
⚡ TRY IT · WHICH LAW JUSTIFIES THIS STEP?
For each situation, choose the best conclusion.
1. Evaluate \( \lim_{x\to 2}(3x^2-5x+1) \) by direct substitution. Which law allows this?
2. Evaluate \( \lim_{x\to 3}\dfrac{x+1}{x-5} \). Can you substitute directly?
3. Which law justifies \( \lim_{x\to a}[f(x)g(x)] = \lim_{x\to a}f(x)\cdot\lim_{x\to a}g(x) \)?
4. \( f \) is continuous with \( \lim_{x\to 1} f(x) = 9 \). Which principle justifies \( \lim_{x\to 1}\sqrt{f(x)} = 3 \)?
▶  Try it in your notebook first: evaluate \( \lim_{x\to -1}\dfrac{2x^2+3}{x+4} \) and name every law you use. Then reveal the check.
The denominator's limit is \(-1+4=3\), which is nonzero, so the quotient law applies. \( \lim_{x\to -1}(2x^2+3) = 2(1)+3 = 5 \) by the sum, product, and constant multiple laws. \( \lim_{x\to -1}(x+4) = 3 \) by the sum law. So the quotient is \( \dfrac{5}{3} \).

💪 THE ONE IDEA TO CARRY FORWARD

The limit laws let you replace patient tables with fast algebra, whenever a function is built from continuous pieces. But the quotient law carries one guardrail: it only applies when the denominator's limit is not zero. When both the numerator and the denominator approach zero at once, substitution goes silent rather than wrong, and a new technique is needed. That is exactly where Lesson 1.03 begins.

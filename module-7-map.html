Implicit Differentiation, Inverses, Logs and Exponentials
What can you differentiate when a curve has no formula for y, or a function has no rule built for its exact shape?
📖 WHAT THIS MODULE IS ABOUT
Lesson 6.04 used a trick without naming it: differentiate both sides of \(\sin y=x\) to find the derivative of \(\arcsin x\). This module names that trick, implicit differentiation, and gives it real room. Lesson 7.01 applies it to curves with no explicit formula for \(y\) at all. Lesson 7.02 turns it on one specific equation, \(f(g(x))=x\), to derive a general rule for the derivative of any inverse function — and checks that rule against Lesson 6.04's own results. Lesson 7.03 puts both tools to work on the single most important inverse pair in mathematics, \(e^x\) and \(\ln x\), finding the same derivative for \(\ln x\) two independent ways and watching them agree. Lesson 7.04 closes the module with logarithmic differentiation, the technique of last resort for a function no single rule reaches directly. By the end of the module, the differentiation toolkit built since Module 4 is genuinely complete: algebraic, trigonometric, inverse, exponential, and logarithmic functions, all in hand.
📅 YOUR LESSONS

💡 HOW TO SUCCEED THIS MODULE
Before you differentiate anything this module, ask one question first: is \(y\) written explicitly in terms of \(x\), or is it hidden inside an equation, an inverse, or an exponent? That single question decides which of the four techniques you reach for. Lesson 7.03 asks you to trust two different derivations of the same result and confirm they agree — do the work of actually checking, rather than assuming it must be fine. Real understanding here looks like knowing which tool a function calls for before you touch a pencil to it, not memorizing four separate procedures in isolation.

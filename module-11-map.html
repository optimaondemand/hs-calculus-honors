Concavity and Curve Sketching
What does the second derivative add, and how do f, f′ and f″ fit into one picture?
📖 WHAT THIS MODULE IS ABOUT
Slope tells you where a curve is going. Concavity tells you how it is bending. Lesson 11.01 defines concave up and concave down through the behavior of \(f'\), and shows why the sign of \(f''\) decides which one you have — the same fact that made the tangent-line estimates in Lesson 9.03 run too high or too low. Lesson 11.02 finds the points where the bending changes, the inflection points, and warns that \(f''=0\) is only a candidate: \(x^4\) has it and no inflection point. It also gives you the Second Derivative Test and says honestly when that test has nothing to say. Lesson 11.03 gathers everything into one checklist — domain, intercepts, symmetry, asymptotes, and the signs of \(f'\) and \(f''\) — and uses it to sketch a polynomial and a rational function. Lesson 11.04 turns the problem around: given a graph or a description of \(f\), \(f'\), or \(f''\), identify the other two and defend your choice, including functions with a corner or a cusp. By the end of the module you will read a graph the way a physician reads an X-ray: slope, bend, and edge cases, all at once.

💡 HOW TO SUCCEED THIS MODULE
Before you sketch anything this module, ask two questions in order: where is the function defined, and what are the signs of \(f'\) and \(f''\) on each piece? Put every point where something is undefined or zero into your sign chart, because those are the only places the graph can change direction or bend. A zero of \(f''\) is a candidate, not a verdict: look at the signs on both sides before you call it an inflection point, and say plainly when a test is inconclusive. Keep your sketches large and labeled, with asymptotes dashed. Real understanding here looks like a graph whose every feature you can justify from the derivatives, not one you copied from a graphing tool. Use technology to check your sketch, never to make it. This module is also where Semester 2's applications begin to pay off, so keep your Week 22 and Week 23 problem sets for later review.

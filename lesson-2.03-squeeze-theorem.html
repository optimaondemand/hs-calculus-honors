📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Trap a function between two others that agree at the target, and it has nowhere else to go.
More than two thousand years ago, Archimedes wanted the area of a circle and had no formula for a curved boundary. His solution was to trap the unknown area between the areas of inscribed and circumscribed polygons, regular shapes he could measure exactly, and add more sides until the two known areas converged on the same number. Whatever lay between them had no room left to be anything else. Today's theorem borrows that exact logic for limits, and uses it to settle one of the most useful limits in this course: what happens to \( \dfrac{\sin x}{x} \) as x approaches zero.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to state the Squeeze Theorem and recognize when a limit can be trapped between two bounding functions.
2
I will be able to explain, using a geometric area argument, why \( \displaystyle\lim_{x\to0}\dfrac{\sin x}{x}=1 \).
3
I will be able to use the special limit to evaluate related limits built from sin x.

📚 PART 1 · THE SQUEEZE (SANDWICH) THEOREM

Suppose \(g(x) \le f(x) \le h(x)\) for every x near a point a, except possibly at a itself, and suppose the two outer functions agree in the limit:

\[ \lim_{x\to a} g(x) = \lim_{x\to a} h(x) = L \]

Then \( \displaystyle\lim_{x\to a} f(x) = L \) as well. The reasoning is exactly Archimedes' polygons: f(x) is squeezed between g(x) and h(x), and if the two bounds are converging on the same number, f(x) has no room to head anywhere else. Notice what the theorem needs: the inequality has to hold near a (a small neighborhood is enough), and the two outer limits have to actually agree. Neither piece can be skipped.

💡 DID YOU KNOW?
Archimedes' method of exhaustion, worked out in the third century B.C., estimated \(\pi\) by squeezing the circle's area between 96-sided inscribed and circumscribed polygons, arriving at bounds accurate to about two decimal places by hand. The same trap-between-two-knowns idea, translated into limit language, is the theorem you are using today.
📚 PART 2 · PROVING SIN X OVER X APPROACHES 1

Direct substitution gives \( \dfrac{\sin 0}{0} = \dfrac{0}{0} \), and no algebra move, factoring or rationalizing, simplifies \(\sin x\) the way it simplified a polynomial. The Squeeze Theorem, aimed at a picture from the unit circle, settles it instead.

Radius \(OA = 1\), angle \(x\) at O, point P on the circle, and T where the tangent at A meets ray OP extended. The three regions: triangle OAP, sector OAP, and triangle OAT, sit inside one another in that order.

For a radius-1 circle and a small positive angle x measured in radians, three regions sit inside one another: the triangle OAP, the circular sector OAP, and the larger triangle OAT formed by the tangent line at A. Their areas are, respectively,

\[ \text{Area}(\triangle OAP) = \tfrac12 \sin x \qquad \text{Area(sector } OAP) = \tfrac12 x \qquad \text{Area}(\triangle OAT) = \tfrac12 \tan x \]

Because the triangle sits inside the sector, which sits inside the larger triangle,

\[ \tfrac12 \sin x \le \tfrac12 x \le \tfrac12 \tan x \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \sin x \le x \le \tan x \]

Divide every part by \(\sin x\), which is positive for \(0 < x < \pi/2\), and the inequalities are preserved:

\[ 1 \le \frac{x}{\sin x} \le \frac{1}{\cos x} \]

Taking reciprocals reverses every inequality:

\[ \cos x \le \frac{\sin x}{x} \le 1 \]

As \(x \to 0^{+}\), \(\cos x \to 1\), so \(\dfrac{\sin x}{x}\) is squeezed between two quantities both approaching 1, and the Squeeze Theorem gives \( \displaystyle\lim_{x\to0^{+}}\dfrac{\sin x}{x}=1 \). Since \(\dfrac{\sin(-x)}{-x} = \dfrac{\sin x}{x}\), the expression is unchanged when x is replaced by \(-x\), so the same limit holds from the left. Both sides agree, giving the standing result

\[ \lim_{x\to0}\frac{\sin x}{x}=1 \]

⚠️ COMMON MISTAKE
This result holds only when x is measured in radians. In degree measure the areas above are not proportional to the angle in the same way, and the limit changes entirely. Whenever you use this special limit, x must be in radians.

Using the special limit

Once \( \displaystyle\lim_{x\to0}\dfrac{\sin x}{x}=1 \) is established, related limits often fall out by matching the argument of sine to its denominator. To evaluate \( \displaystyle\lim_{x\to0}\dfrac{\sin 5x}{2x} \), multiply and divide to expose the special form:

\[ \lim_{x\to0}\frac{\sin 5x}{2x} = \lim_{x\to0}\left(\frac{\sin 5x}{5x}\cdot\frac{5x}{2x}\right) = \lim_{x\to0}\frac{\sin 5x}{5x}\cdot\lim_{x\to0}\frac{5}{2} \]

\[ = 1 \cdot \frac{5}{2} = \frac{5}{2} \]

📚 PART 3 · SQUEEZING A DIFFERENT KIND OF FUNCTION

The theorem is not only for trig limits. It works anywhere you can trap a function between two others that converge on the same value, which is especially useful for functions that oscillate wildly, like \(\sin(1/x)\), where direct reasoning about the oscillation is hard but a bound is easy.

▶  Try it in your notebook first: since \(-1 \le \sin(1/x) \le 1\) for every \(x \ne 0\), find bounding functions for \( f(x) = x^2\sin(1/x) \) and use the Squeeze Theorem to evaluate \( \displaystyle\lim_{x\to0} x^2\sin(1/x) \). Then reveal the check.
Multiply the inequality \(-1 \le \sin(1/x) \le 1\) by \(x^2\), which is never negative, so the direction of the inequality does not flip: \(-x^2 \le x^2\sin(1/x) \le x^2\). Both outer functions satisfy \( \displaystyle\lim_{x\to0}(-x^2)=0 \) and \( \displaystyle\lim_{x\to0}x^2=0 \), so by the Squeeze Theorem, \( \displaystyle\lim_{x\to0} x^2\sin(1/x) = 0 \), even though \(\sin(1/x)\) itself oscillates endlessly and has no limit of its own as \(x\to0\).

Explain to a classmate why it is the squared factor \(x^2\), rather than the oscillating factor \(\sin(1/x)\), that decides this limit.

💪 THE ONE IDEA TO CARRY FORWARD

When a function is trapped between two others that agree on the same limit, it has nowhere else to go. That single idea, ancient enough to have measured circles before calculus existed, is strong enough to establish \( \displaystyle\lim_{x\to0}\dfrac{\sin x}{x}=1 \) and to tame oscillating functions no algebra move can touch directly. Keep this special limit; you will use it again.

Q3 · MODULE 12 · WEEKS 24, 25 AND 26
Optimization, Motion, and Related Rates
How do you turn a story about the world into a function, and then let calculus find the best case or the rate of change?
📖 WHAT THIS MODULE IS ABOUT
Everything this quarter has built converges on one move: model a real situation, then let the derivative find the answer. Every earlier module handed you a function. This module hands you a story — a farmer with 200 meters of fence, a ladder sliding down a wall, a particle on a line — and asks you to build the function yourself. Lessons 12.01 and 12.02 teach applied optimization: draw, name the variables, write the quantity to be made largest or smallest, use a constraint to reduce it to one variable, state the domain, and then justify that the answer is truly the best case, not just a critical point. Lesson 12.03 returns to motion along a line, building on Lesson 8.02 with sign charts, total distance, and the difference between displacement and distance. Lesson 12.04 closes the module with related rates: when two quantities are tied together by a relationship and both change with time, differentiate the relationship with respect to time. Module 12 runs three weeks because it is the heaviest applications module of the year: optimization takes Week 24, motion takes Week 25, and related rates takes Week 26. Pace yourself, and let the sketches do the work.

💡 HOW TO SUCCEED THIS MODULE
Most of the work in this module happens before you take a single derivative. Draw the picture, name every quantity, and write down which one you want to make largest or smallest and which relationship ties the others to it. Then, before you trust an answer, ask whether it is truly the best case: check the endpoints of the domain and say why the critical point wins. In related rates, remember the one rule that trips up nearly everyone — differentiate first, substitute the numbers afterward. Real understanding here looks like being able to say, in words, what your answer means and what assumptions it rests on, not only having a number at the bottom of the page.

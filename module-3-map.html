Q1 · MODULE 3 · WEEKS 5 AND 6
Continuity & the Great Theorems
What does it mean for a function to have no gaps, and what can we prove because of it?
📖 WHAT THIS MODULE IS ABOUT
Over these two weeks you will pin down, precisely, what it means for a function to have no gaps. You will learn the three-part definition of continuity, sort every kind of gap into one of three named families, and then discover why continuity is worth defining so carefully in the first place: it is the exact hypothesis that unlocks two of the great theorems of calculus, the Intermediate Value Theorem and the Extreme Value Theorem. Both theorems promise that something exists, a root, a maximum, a minimum, without ever showing you how to find it. Keep your eye on the fine print the whole time: every one of these guarantees depends on hypotheses that must actually be checked, not assumed.

💡 HOW TO SUCCEED THIS MODULE
State plainly which of the three conditions fails whenever a function is not continuous, rather than just saying it "looks broken." When you use the Intermediate Value Theorem or the Extreme Value Theorem, name both hypotheses out loud and check that each one actually holds before you claim the guarantee. That precision, and the honesty to say when a guarantee does not apply, is what this module is asking of you.
